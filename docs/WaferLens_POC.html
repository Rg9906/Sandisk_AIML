Section 01

Executive summary

WaferLens predicts which semiconductor dies that passed pre-test are about to fail, using three resolutions of evidence at once: the die's own 500 electrical parametrics, its position and neighbourhood on the wafer, and the 2,000 sub-die block readings taken inside it. It reaches 0.5767 AUC-PR against a best incumbent method of 0.0734 — a 7.9× improvement — and returns, for every die, a calibrated probability, an uncertainty interval, a three-way attribution of where the risk came from, and a named physical root cause with the fab module to inspect.

The five things that matter

  1. The hackathon's core question is answered, with a number. The 2,000 block readings do help, but modestly: +0.0291 AUC-PR, a 5.3% relative gain. We report the size of the effect rather than claiming a breakthrough.
  2. We implemented the incumbent methods first. DPAT, GDBN and NNR are the screens the industry uses today. Without them, "0.58 AUC-PR" means nothing. With them, it means 7.9×.
  3. Every prediction is decomposed by resolution. Not just which features mattered, but how much of the risk came from the die, from its neighbourhood, and from inside it. That is what turns a probability into an engineering instruction.
  4. Probabilities are calibrated and carry intervals. Brier score improves from 0.0640 raw to 0.0249 after isotonic regression; expected calibration error falls to 0.0056. A stated risk of 0.30 really does mean three in ten.
  5. Our negative results are in the document. The multi-scale spatial factory cost us 0.008 AUC-PR. Per-wafer adaptive weighting under-performed global weighting. SMOTE hurt. Venn-ABERS intervals were degenerate. The attention branch under-performed statistical pooling on the shipped run. All of it is measured and reported.

What a judge can do in five minutes

Run python -m webapp.server and open the console. Press 1 for the overview, then click any die on the wafer map to see its risk decomposed three ways. Press 5 and drag the decision threshold to watch recall and yield loss trade against each other on the real test set. Press 2 and start the pipeline: it trains, calibrates and redraws every figure in front of you in under two minutes.

Section 02

Problem and business context

A wafer is a disc of silicon carrying roughly a thousand near-identical dies in a grid. After fabrication every die is electrically probed and marked pass or fail. Some dies are already known-failed going into this round of testing; those carry old_label == 1. The rest look fine.

The task is to predict which of the currently-passing dies will fail. A die that passes probe but fails later becomes a field failure, a return, and in the worst case a recall. A die scrapped unnecessarily is pure yield loss. The screen sits exactly between those two costs.

Why the incumbent methods are running out of road

The industry ladder is PAT → DPAT → NNR → GDBN. GDBN — "Good Die in a Bad Neighborhood" — scraps a die that tests fine but sits surrounded by failures. These methods assume univariate Gaussian parameter distributions and threshold one parameter at a time. At advanced nodes that assumption breaks down, and our own data reproduces the published critique: the median Cohen's d across the 500 parametrics is 0.134. There is no single parameter for a univariate rule to threshold on.

Our m × m neighbourhood window is a learned replacement for that heuristic, and the block branch adds a resolution the incumbent methods do not look at at all.

Where the value sits

Failure modeConsequenceWho pays
Escape (missed failure)Field failure, RMA, possible recallCustomer, then warranty
Overkill (false alarm)A good die is scrappedYield, directly
Undiagnosed excursionThe fault repeats on the next lotEvery wafer until it is found

The third row is the one a probability alone cannot address, and the reason this proof of concept invests as heavily in attribution as in accuracy.

The metric decision, made before any modelling

Only 4.23% of eligible dies newly fail. A model that predicts "everything passes" scores 95.77% accuracy and catches nothing. Accuracy is therefore reported once, because the deliverable asks for it, and is never used to select anything. Selection is on AUC-PR on the failure class, with F1 as the secondary check.

Splitting, and the trap we found

Splits are made by wafer, never by die. Dies from one wafer share a process history, so splitting by die leaks that history across the boundary and inflates every score. The pipeline asserts zero wafer overlap on every run.

Trap found in the supplied kit

The kit's validation.parquet is test.parquet with the label column dropped — the same wafers, an identical row count. It is not a third split. Tuning on it would be tuning on the test set. We carve validation out of train by wafer and keep test as a true holdout, using validation.parquet only to produce the submission file.

Section 06

Reconnaissance: five measurements that set the design

Before writing a single model we spent two hours measuring the data. Each of the five answers changed an architectural decision. This is the section that explains why the system looks the way it does.

QuestionMeasured answerWhat it changed
Q1 · Failure rate among eligible dies 4.18%, inside the stated 2–5% band. All-pass scores ~95.8% accuracy. Select on AUC-PR. Never report accuracy as a result.
Q2 · Are block readings ordered? Ordered. Lag-1 autocorrelation 0.250 against a shuffled control of +0.001, decaying to zero by lag 5 — the footprint of a smoothing kernel. Run-length features become meaningful and attention has a genuine contiguous cluster to point at.
Q3 · Spatial ↔ parametric correlation Weak. The 5×5 bad-neighbour ratio correlates +0.012 with the new-failure label pooled, +0.018 centred within wafer. Between wafers the correlation is −0.187, which drags the pooled figure negative. Build neighbourhood features, but expect little. The wafer-level rate carries the spatial information — which the ablation later confirmed.
Q4 · Are wafers cleanly separated? Only because we enforce it. All three pairwise overlaps are zero. The kit's validation file mirrors test. By-wafer split with an assertion, and a carved validation set.
Q5 · Class overlap Severe. Median Cohen's d 0.133; the best single feature reaches 0.369. But the features are near-independent, so they add in quadrature to an aggregate d = 3.44. The single most consequential finding. Do not hope a tree finds one good feature among 500 — build an explicit multivariate anomaly score that pools all of them.

Q3 also measured the bad-neighbour ratio against parametric anomaly at +0.015 — effectively zero. Neighbourhood badness does not announce itself in a die's own parameters, so the two channels are independent sources of evidence. That is the argument for fusing them rather than expecting one to subsume the other.

Section 07

Feature engineering

Roughly 2,500 raw inputs per die are reduced to 88 engineered features across three resolutions. Every transform is fitted on train only and applied unchanged to validation and test.

2,500 raw inputs per die ├── 500 parametrics ──► DIE features ── per-wafer z-scores, robust scaling, │ pooled Mahalanobis-style anomaly score ├── grid position ──► SPATIAL features ── m×m bad-neighbour ratio, radial position, │ directional asymmetry, longest run, │ transductive wafer prior, signature class └── 2,000 blocks ──► BLOCK features ── 25 summary statistics, hot-block count, longest contiguous excursion, autocorrelation ──► 88 features ──► model

Die features

Parametrics are z-scored within each wafer, so a die is measured against its own lot rather than a global mean. The pooled anomaly score is the direct answer to recon Q5: it combines all 500 weak, near-independent signals into one strong feature instead of asking a tree to find a needle. That single feature is worth +0.0278 AUC-PR in the ablation.

Spatial features

The neighbourhood window generalises GDBN: instead of a hand-set bad-neighbour threshold, the model learns how much neighbourhood evidence to weigh. Alongside it sit radial position, directional asymmetry and the longest run of failures — the shape descriptors that make a signature identifiable. The transductive wafer prior, which estimates each wafer's own failure rate, is the largest single spatial win at +0.0463 AUC-PR.

Block features

Twenty-five summary statistics per die, computed vectorised over the memory-mapped float16 cache. Because recon Q2 proved the readings are ordered, run-length statistics — the longest contiguous stretch above the die's own mean plus two standard deviations — are meaningful rather than arbitrary. Feeding 2,000 raw values per die directly to a model was never viable at 32,598 dies.

Performance constraint that shaped the code

No per-die Python loop over the wafer survives at this scale. Every spatial feature is computed with scipy.ndimage convolutions or vectorised NumPy over the whole wafer grid at once.

A gradient-boosted decision tree (LightGBM) over the 63 die and spatial features. Model B — adding the 2,000 block readings

Model B was built as two independent branches over the same dies, so that the contribution of the block resolution could be measured rather than assumed.

BranchArchitectureAUC-PRF1
Model A, for referenceLightGBM over die + spatial0.54760.5214
Branch 1 — statistical poolingLightGBM over die + spatial + 25 block aggregates0.57670.5382
Branch 2 — gated attention-MILPyTorch, 2,000 blocks as a multi-instance bag0.27290.4451

Shipped model: Branch 1. On this run the attention branch under-performed and was not blended into the shipped predictions. See section 18.

The attention branch, and why it is still in the repository

The attention-MIL branch treats the 2,000 readings as a bag of instances and learns a gated attention weight per chunk, tanh(V) ⊙ σ(U), so the model itself says which stretch of blocks drove its decision. That makes block interpretability architectural rather than a post-hoc SHAP approximation over 2,000 dimensions. On earlier runs it reached 0.5986 AUC-PR and blended productively; on the shipped run it did not. We keep it, report both outcomes, and ship the branch that measured better.

The answer to the hackathon's core question

Yes, the block readings help — by +0.0291 AUC-PR, a 5.31% relative gain. That is a real improvement and a modest one. It arrives through statistical pooling of the 2,000 readings, not through the attention architecture. Precision improves most: 0.637 → 0.678, meaning the block signal mainly removes false alarms rather than finding new failures.

Section 10

Class imbalance — six rungs, measured after each

Deliverable 5 asks how each model behaves under a 2–5% failure rate with overlapping distributions. We ran a ladder of six strategies and measured after every rung rather than reporting only the winner.

StrategyAUC-PRF1RecallPrecisionCut

What actually moved the needle

  1. Threshold tuning is the biggest lever, and it is free. Rung 1 to rung 2 changes no model at all — only where the probability is cut. F1 moves 0.4230 → 0.5491 at an identical AUC-PR of 0.5859. Ranking quality was never the problem; the default cut point was.
  2. Class weighting trades precision for recall; it adds no information. Rung 0 to rung 1 lifts recall from 0.371 to 0.617 while AUC-PR barely moves. Reaching for class_weight='balanced' and stopping there relabels the same ranking.
  3. Focal loss won. It down-weights easy examples so the gradient budget reaches the ambiguous overlap, which is where 65% of this dataset's failures sit by construction.
  4. Per-wafer adaptive weighting did not pay off (0.5729 against 0.5859). We expected it to win. The likely reason is that the transductive wafer-prior features already give the model each wafer's rate, so re-encoding it in the sample weights is redundant.
  5. SMOTE was run last and hurt (0.5607). Synthetic minority points are interpolated between existing failures, and at a median Cohen's d of 0.134 those interpolations land inside the pass distribution. Manufacturing more points inside the overlap cannot resolve the overlap.

Calibration and uncertainty

A probability an operator cannot price is not useful. Isotonic regression is fitted on the validation wafers only and applied unchanged to test.

MeasureRawCalibratedMeaning

Per-die intervals

We implemented Venn-ABERS first, because it is the textbook distribution-free answer, then measured it and found it degenerate at this sample size.

MethodMean widthBand coverageVerdict
Venn-ABERS (IVAP)0.001711%Degenerate — width shrinks as O(1/n)
Bootstrap over the calibration set0.012167%Shipped

With a 29,532-die calibration set, the two isotonic fits behind IVAP differ by one point in 29,532, so the interval collapses. It answers "how much could one more observation move this estimate", which is not the question an engineer is asking. Resampling the calibration set answers the right question and brackets the observed failure rate in 67% of risk bands. We report both and ship the bootstrap interval.

Section 12

Threshold and economics

The decision threshold is not a modelling choice; it is a business one. The pipeline sweeps every candidate cut over the held-out test set and prices each one, given a cost ratio between shipping a bad die and scrapping a good one.

Operating pointCutRecallYield lossEscape rate
Shipped, balanced0.283344.6%2.79%55.4%
Cost-optimal at ratio 1000.034480.7%22.8%19.3%
Cost-optimal at ratio 200.055571.8%13.6%28.2%
GDBN incumbent, its own optimum—7.0%—93.0%

At a cost ratio of 100 — one escape costs as much as a hundred scrapped dies — the optimal cut reduces expected failure cost by 76.1% against shipping every die, worth roughly $7,900 per wafer at $2.50 per die.

Read the annualised figure carefully

Extrapolated to 20,000 wafers a month the modelled saving is large, but it is the product of four operator-supplied assumptions: cost ratio, die cost, volume, and the acceptable scrap ceiling. The console exposes all four as sliders precisely so nobody has to take a single headline number on trust. The defensible claim is the percentage, not the currency.

Section 14

Interpretability

Deliverable 4 asks for per-die feature importance, a spatial contribution visualisation, and block pattern analysis. We treat interpretability as the product rather than a reporting obligation.

Die (14, 22) — failure risk 0.83 [interval 0.71–0.91] ├── 0.31 DIE IDDQ sits 3.2σ above its spatially-expected value ├── 0.28 SPATIAL neighbourhood matches an Edge-Ring signature (5×5 BNR = 0.68) └── 0.24 BLOCK attention concentrated on blocks 1420–1610 ↳ ROOT CAUSE RTP / anneal temperature regulation ↳ ACTION screen die; flag lot for thermal-module review

Three layers of explanation

  1. Attribution by resolution. Signed tree-SHAP values are grouped into die, spatial and block families and normalised to sum to one. This answers a question raw feature importance cannot: which level of the hierarchy is responsible.
  2. Signed per-feature contributions. The top five features for the die, with direction, so an engineer can see what pushed the risk up and what pulled it down.
  3. Block pattern analysis. The 2,000-reading waveform with the longest contiguous excursion above the die's own mean plus two standard deviations marked, plus the attention weights from the MIL branch where available.

Signature to physical root cause

The spatial signature classifier is a deliberately transparent rule set over the spatial features, not a model. Every branch can be audited, and it needs no training data. Each signature maps to a documented physical cause and a specific engineering action.

Engineering discipline

  • Every run appends a row to results/results.csv. An experiment that was not logged did not happen.
  • Seeds are set for NumPy, Python, PyTorch and every model's random_state.
  • Nothing is fitted on validation or test — not scalers, thresholds, imputers or class weights.
  • The supplied generator script is read and wrapped, never modified.

Section 18

Negative results and limitations

A proof of concept that reports only its wins is not evidence. These are measured, reproducible, and in the repository.

What we expectedWhat we measuredWhat we did
A richer multi-scale spatial factory would help−0.0079 AUC-PRReported it; kept the features for interpretability, not for score
Neighbourhood context would carry the spatial signal+0.005; the wafer rate carried it at +0.0463Rebuilt around the transductive wafer prior

SMOTE would help a rare class0.5607, the worst rungRan it last, as predicted, and reported the harm

Known limitations

  • Escape rate is 55.4% at the shipped cut. More than half of real failures are still missed. The economics sweep shows this is a chosen operating point, not a ceiling — recall reaches 80.7% at a 22.8% yield cost — but the shipped point is conservative.
  • The A→B delta is not significance-tested. +0.0291 AUC-PR on one held-out split is a point estimate. Repeated splits or a bootstrap over wafers would be the next step.
  • Single dataset, single generator configuration. Nothing here demonstrates generalisation to a different fab, node or product.
  • Attention-MIL results are unstable across runs, ranging from 0.27 to 0.60 AUC-PR. That variance is itself a finding and needs seed-averaging before the branch could be trusted.
  • Root-cause mapping is a rule set, not a validated diagnosis. It encodes published signature-to-module associations; it has not been confirmed against real fab failure analysis.

Section 22

Reproducing this work

python -m src.recon --dataset full # the five proofs python -m src.report --dataset full # full pipeline, all artifacts python -m src.ablation --dataset full # component waterfall python -m src.imbalance --dataset full # the six-rung ladder python -m webapp.server # console at 127.0.0.1:8000

Every command appends its results to results/results.csv with a timestamp, so any figure in this document can be traced to the run that produced it.

Run provenance for this document

All figures come from the full-dataset run backing results/preds_full.pkl — 40 held-out wafers, 39,351 dies, 32,598 eligible, 1,380 new failures, shipped model Model B (statistical pooling) at a calibrated threshold of 0.2833. This is the same artifact that produces submission.csv and drives every number in the live console, so the document, the submission file and the demo cannot disagree.

Dependencies

The supplied kit requires NumPy, pandas, SciPy, PyYAML, PyArrow, scikit-learn and Matplotlib. We added LightGBM, scikit-image, SHAP, Optuna, imbalanced-learn and PyTorch for the attention branch. The console adds nothing — it is standard-library Python and hand-written CSS and JavaScript, deliberately, so that the demo can never fail on a dependency.
